# Evidence And CRO Sources

Use these sources when a design decision needs conversion, UX, or product-flow justification.

## CRO And Landing Optimization

### CXL

Source: https://cxl.com/blog/landing-page-optimization/

Use for:

- message match between traffic source and landing page;
- landing page optimization process;
- conversion research framing;
- copy-first page planning.

Do not use as:

- a visual style source;
- proof that a specific design pattern always converts.

## E-commerce And Product Page UX

### Baymard

Sources:

- https://baymard.com/
- https://baymard.com/research/product-page

Use for:

- product page UX;
- checkout, product detail, filtering, search and e-commerce evidence;
- benchmark-style UX reasoning.

Do not use as:

- a free replacement for premium Baymard reports;
- a reason to invent product metrics.

## Product Psychology And UX Case Studies

### Growth.Design

Sources:

- https://growth.design/
- https://growth.design/case-studies

Use for:

- product psychology;
- onboarding, retention, revenue and referral case studies;
- explaining user perception and behavior.

Do not use as:

- a component source;
- a reason to make every section playful or comic-like.

## Heuristic Evaluation

### Nielsen Norman Group

Source: https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ten-usability-heuristics/

Use for:

- visibility of system status;
- match with real-world user language;
- consistency and standards;
- recognition over recall;
- error prevention and recovery.

### Laws of UX

Source: https://lawsofux.com/

Use for:

- quick psychology checks;
- cognitive load and choice overload checks;
- design rationale vocabulary.

Do not use as:

- strict law;
- substitute for testing or page-specific evidence.

## How To Use Sources In A Plan

For every research-backed decision, write:

- Source:
- Principle:
- Landing section:
- Applied decision:
- Evidence strength: strong / medium / weak / inferred.
- What could invalidate it:

Example:

```text
Source: CXL landing-page optimization.
Principle: message match between source intent and page promise.
Section: hero.
Applied decision: hero headline mirrors the specific campaign/user problem instead of a generic brand slogan.
Evidence strength: medium.
What could invalidate it: organic homepage traffic with broad intent.
```
